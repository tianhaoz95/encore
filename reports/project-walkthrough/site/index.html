Project walkthrough

Your clip ends.
Encore keeps going.

Give Encore a movie clip and one sentence, such as “a K-pop concert → a backstage podcast with the members”. It writes, casts, voices, shoots and edits the follow-up scene. Every model runs on a Mac Studio and a DGX Spark on your desk, with no cloud APIs.

01 · The idea

Not one model. A film crew.

No open video model can watch a clip and keep going for minutes in a new scenario. So Encore splits the job the way a production does. Each department is a different local model, and the code coordinates them.

Research

Understand

Find the cuts, separate vocals from the music, transcribe speech, and group faces into people. A vision model describes the clip's look.

Writers' room

Write

A local LLM turns the cast list and your request into a strict JSON shot list. Each shot has a type, framing, setting, prompt and dialogue.

Casting

Cast

Each person gets face references, an appearance description and a voice sample cut from the clip. Those become identity and voice conditioning.

Camera

Shoot

Keyframes come first, because stills are cheap to fix. Talking shots then get audio-driven lip sync; b-roll and continuations get image-to-video.

Post

Edit

Every shot is conformed and color-matched to the source with a 3D LUT. Then come film grain, loudness normalization, subtitles and a watermark.

Director (you)

Review

Every artifact is a file. Rename people, fix lines, or regenerate a single keyframe or shot without redoing anything else.

02 · Architecture

A brain and a camera on one LAN

The Mac Studio is the brain. Its large unified memory and MLX make it ideal for big LLMs, the vision model, Whisper and the UI. The DGX Spark is the camera. Nearly every video, lip-sync and voice-cloning repo assumes CUDA, so all diffusion work runs there. The animated lines show where requests go.

your requests local calls on the Mac HTTP to the Spark (ComfyUI / worker)

Why not run everything on one machine?

The Mac is excellent for large models: MLX streams tokens fast thanks to ~800 GB/s of memory bandwidth. But PyTorch on Apple's GPU backend (MPS) is slow for video diffusion, and many custom CUDA kernels simply don't run on it. The Spark is the reverse: CUDA, FP8/FP4 and every repo works, but its 273 GB/s bandwidth makes huge LLMs slower.

Every stage is movable

Each backend is just a URL in config.yaml. Run TTS on the Mac, move the LLM to the Spark with vLLM, or use only one box. For a no-GPU dry run, swap everything for fallbacks with -s key=value overrides or configs/dryrun.yaml.

03 · Pipeline

Seven stages, each one resumable

Click a stage, or press play to walk through a run. Each stage reads the previous stage's files and writes its own, and project.json records its status. If a run fails at the shoot stage, it resumes there.

04 · Data model

Everything is a file you can open

Stages communicate only through Pydantic models saved as JSON (encore/core/schemas.py). The two you'll touch are the Story Bible (who is in the clip) and the Shot List (what to film).

An example shot list on a timeline

Block widths are proportional to duration. Press play to scrub through the final cut: the source clip comes first, then the generated shots, with dialogue and subtitles underneath.

    ],
    "duration": 6.4  // rewritten to match the audio
  }]
}
LLM output is never trusted as-is. scriptwriter.normalize() repairs common slips: names used instead of ids, speakers missing from characters, duplicate ids, an extend shot that isn't first, and talking shots with no lines. The client first requests a json_schema response, falls back to json_object and then plain text, and feeds validation errors back to the model for up to two retries.
05 · Deep dives

The six hard problems and how each is handled

① Who is in the clip? Face clustering

Frames are sampled at 2 fps. Every face is detected and turned into a 512-d identity embedding (ArcFace via InsightFace, or OpenCV's YuNet + SFace as a fallback). Average-linkage agglomerative clustering on cosine distance then merges the closest groups until no pair is closer than faces.cluster_threshold (0.45). Groups smaller than 3 faces count as noise, such as crowd members.

For each person, the 6 best crops (scored on detection confidence, size and sharpness, and spread across time) become the face references. The average embedding is saved for identity checks later.

Code: encore/analyze/faces.py → cluster_embeddings(), stages/bible.py → _pick_refs()

② What do they sound like? Voices and per-speaker tracks

Voice references. Concert audio is mostly music, so Demucs separates the vocals first. Each transcript segment is assigned to a character. With diarization, speaker labels map to characters by how often that face is on screen while the speaker talks. Without it, each segment goes to the largest face on screen. Up to 12 s of a character's longest segments become voice_ref.wav.

Dialogue. Each line is synthesized separately and cached by a hash of its text, voice and emotion, so editing one line re-renders only that line. Lines are laid out with a 0.3 s lead-in and 0.35 s gaps. One extra track per on-screen character carries their lines and is silent elsewhere. Multi-person lip-sync models (MultiTalk/InfiniteTalk, audio_type: "para") need exactly this to drive the right mouth.

③ Keeping faces consistent: master frames and identity QC

Generating every shot from text alone gives everyone a slightly different face in every shot. Instead, per setting:

  1. Generate one wide master frame containing everyone, conditioned on a stitched sheet of their face references. FLUX Kontext edits from reference images.
  2. Make every other shot in that setting an edit of the master (“close-up of the woman on the left…”), conditioned on the master plus that character's face reference. The room, lighting and wardrobe carry over.
  3. Start extend shots from the source clip's actual last frame, so the cut is seamless.

Each candidate is then checked: InsightFace finds the faces, and each expected character's best cosine similarity is averaged. A candidate below image.identity_threshold is regenerated with a new seed, up to 3 attempts, and the best one is kept.

④ Shots longer than the model can make: chaining

Image-to-video models produce about 5 s per run (Wan needs 4n+1 frames, e.g. 121 frames at 24 fps). For longer b-roll, VideoGen.motion() splits the shot into equal segments. Each segment starts from the previous segment's last frame, and the segments are concatenated.

Talking shots are handled differently. InfiniteTalk runs in streaming mode with overlapping motion frames, so a long dialogue becomes one continuous lip-synced take.

⑤ Making new shots look like the source: LUT color matching

Generated shots usually look cleaner and flatter than real concert footage. In the edit stage, Encore measures the mean and standard deviation of each shot in CIE Lab space and compares them with the source clip's last scenes. It then applies a Reinhard transfer:

lab' = (lab − μ_shot) · clamp(σ_src/σ_shot, 0.5, 2) + μ_src
out  = lab + (lab' − lab) · strength      # strength 0.7

Instead of processing frames in Python, the transform is baked into a 33³ 3D LUT (.cube) and applied by ffmpeg's lut3d filter, which is fast. Drag the slider to try it on a palette, using the same math in JavaScript.

⑥ Putting it together: the final edit

Every part is conformed to one format (default 1280×720 at 25 fps, H.264/AAC). If a generated video is shorter than its dialogue, the last frame is held (tpad) so lines are never cut off. The final ffmpeg pass:

Optional music bed: the source's instrumental stem is looped under the new footage at −18 dB and ducked with sidechaincompress whenever someone speaks. Then loudnorm brings it to −16 LUFS. A watermark is overlaid and comment=AI-generated… is written to the MP4 metadata.

08 · Testing guide

Test it layer by layer

Start with zero dependencies and add one real component at a time. If something breaks, you'll know which layer caused it. Levels 0–3 run on any Mac with ffmpeg. Levels 4–6 need the Spark and the Studio.

11 tests covering JSON extraction, script repair, the LLM client's schema fallback and retry, ComfyUI templating with a fake ComfyUI over HTTP, face clustering, LUT math, a full dry-run pipeline, single-shot regeneration, and a live worker round-trip.
